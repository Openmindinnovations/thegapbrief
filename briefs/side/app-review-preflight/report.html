Pre-Flight for Vibe Coders: An App Store Rejection Scanner and 5 Sibling Ideas

People building apps with Cursor, Lovable, Bolt, Replit, Rork and v0 are reaching App Review in large numbers. This report asks whether a “check before you submit” service is still open, and finds a market that is real, busy and cheap.

Assigned by Jacob Peterson. Every factual claim links to its source. Competitor prices are as shown on the vendors’ pages when checked on October 11, 2026 (or as indexed, where marked). No market sizes, revenue, user counts or conversion rates are estimated. Suggested prices are our proposals and are labeled. Not affiliated with Apple or Google; “App Store” and “Google Play” are their trademarks.

SummaryBottom line and ranking

The demand is documented by Apple itself. It reviewed 9,100,620 submissions in 2025 and rejected 2,093,244, and it says that on average over 40% of unresolved issues fall under guideline 2.1, App Completeness. The catch is competition. At least seven pre-submission checkers already sell for $2.99 to $199, and the free, open-source fastlane precheck covers metadata. A new entrant can’t win on “we check your app.” It has to win on who it serves and what it checks that others don’t. Vibe coders ship a web wrapper, a Supabase backend and an AI feature at once, and no single tool we found checks all three against both store rules and data exposure.

Why the main concept ranks #1 despite a lower score than #2

On the scores alone, the privacy checker edges ahead because the field is less crowded. We still rank the full pre-flight first. It has the strongest cited demand, and the privacy and security checks are better sold as modules inside it than as separate products. The honest launch order is: build #2 and #3 as the scan engine first, then market the whole thing as #1.

Overlap with Jacob’s projects: none of the six is a UI generator, so they don’t overlap with Get UI Now. We deliberately left out “generate store screenshots and listings,” which would edge toward it. The launch-readiness metadata check survives only as a check inside #1. Nothing here repackages Kapsoul, iMusement, Hivvly, FlirtFee, Anchor AR, Wash Wizardz, DeepReach or Fetch.

EvidenceWhat the stores say, what changed, who is already here

E.1Apple’s own numbers (2025 Transparency Report)

  • 9,100,620 submissions reviewed; 2,093,244 rejected; 387,087 approved after an earlier rejection.
  • Rejections by guideline section (one submission can break several): Performance 1,354,418 · Legal 495,673 · Design 415,532 · Business 283,820 · Safety 151,159 · Other 4,145.
  • Apple’s newsroom adds: over 1.2 million of the rejections were new apps and nearly 800,000 were updates. Over 443,000 were for privacy violations, over 371,000 for copying, spam or misleading users, and over 22,000 for hidden or undocumented features.
  • Apple’s App Review page: “On average, over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1: App Completeness.”
  • Google doesn’t publish submission counts. Its February 2026 post says it kept more than 1.75 million policy-violating apps from being published in 2025 (as summarized by AskSami).

E.2Guideline changes that matter to AI-built apps (2025–2026)

  • Nov 13, 2025: 5.1.2(i) now says you must disclose where personal data is shared “with third-party AI” and get explicit permission. New 4.1(c) bars using another developer’s icon, brand or product name. 4.7 now covers HTML5/JS mini apps.
  • June 8, 2026: Apple clarified the basis for 4.3(a) and 4.3(b) (spam and saturated categories) and added examples. It also added new developer responsibilities under 1.2.
  • May 2025 (US storefront only): after the Epic ruling, 3.1.1(a) lets apps link to outside purchase methods without an entitlement. Other storefronts keep their own rules.
  • Long-standing rules vibe builds keep hitting: 4.2 minimum functionality (“beyond a repackaged website”), 4.2.6 apps from “app generation services,” and 2.5.2, which bars downloading or running code that changes features. In March 2026, Apple blocked updates for vibe-coding apps Replit and Vibecode and pulled Anything. Apple told The Information it was enforcing “long-standing rules,” not targeting vibe coding.

E.3Vibe coders hitting rejections

  • r/vibecoding thread “Apple rejecting vibe coded apps?” (March 2026): a builder worried about launch; replies separate crackdowns on vibe-coding tools (2.5.2) from rejections of poorly finished apps.
  • Lovable-to-native shops say a bare Capacitor wrapper “usually does not” pass 4.2. lovable2app calls 4.2 “the single most common rejection we see on Lovable apps,” often alongside 4.8 (Sign in with Apple).
  • Agency guides list the same families: 2.1, 4.2, 4.2.6, 4.3, 5.1, 3.1.1 and 2.5.2. These are vendor posts, not neutral data.

MainDeep dive: a pre-flight scanner for vibe coders

M.1Target audience and positioning

WhoFirst-time App Store submitters who built with Lovable, Bolt, v0 or Replit (web apps wrapped with Capacitor) or with Rork, Expo or Cursor (React Native). They don’t read Xcode warnings and won’t open a terminal.

WedgeCompetitors scan an Xcode project, a CLI build or App Store Connect metadata. The wedge here is to start from where vibe coders are: a GitHub repo or builder export, the live web URL and the Supabase project. Then check the three things that sink these builds together: the wrapper (4.2), the backend (exposed data), and privacy and AI consent (5.1). Findings come back as copy-paste fix prompts for the builder the user already works in.

M.2Core features and scan flow

  • 1. Connect a GitHub repo, builder export zip or .ipa, plus the live URL. Optional: read-only App Store Connect key (AppClearance takes the same read-only approach).
  • 2. Deterministic checks, each mapped to a guideline number: permissions and purpose strings, privacy manifest, Sign in with Apple, account deletion, placeholder content, dead links, remote code and OTA patterns, WebView-only detection with a “native surface” count, external purchase links by storefront.
  • 3. Backend probe of the user’s own deployed app, with proof of ownership: anon-key reads on tables, RLS gaps, service keys in the bundle.
  • 4. LLM pass with labeled confidence: screenshots vs. app, description vs. features, AI disclosure and consent screen, “looks like many other apps” (4.3) warning.
  • 5. Output: a readiness score, blockers and risks, the guideline cited for each, fix prompts for Lovable/Cursor/Rork, a reviewer-notes draft and a demo-account checklist.

M.3Funnel and pricing

FunnelFree scan showing the score, counts and the top 3 findings (the same pattern ReviewAhead and VAS use). Then a one-time full report with fix prompts. Then a subscription that re-scans each release, watches the backend and alerts on guideline changes.

ComparablesReports sell for $2.99 (ReviewAhead) to $49 (ReviewReady AI). Subscriptions run $4.99/mo (ReviewAhead) to $29/mo (AppClearance, Appflight Solo). Human review costs $199, and rejection rescue $399 (ReviewReady).

SuggestedOur suggestion: $19 per full report, because it bundles the security and privacy modules that cheap checkers lack. $15/mo for one app with release re-scans and backend monitoring. An optional $149 human pass, sold only when a reviewer is available. These are untested proposals.

M.4Low-touch maintenance

  • Automated: a daily diff of Apple’s guidelines page and developer news, Google Play policy pages, and the Lovable and Expo changelogs. Each diff opens a “rule review” ticket.
  • Human, a few hours a month (estimate): turn guideline diffs into rule updates, triage false positives, refresh the fix-prompt templates when builder UIs change.
  • No human in the paid path: scans, reports and billing are self-serve. Human review is opt-in and capped.

M.5Moat and risks

  • Platform risk: Apple or a builder could ship this. Lovable already gives away security scans, Apple is adding agentic coding to Xcode, and fastlane precheck is free. The moat is thin: cross-builder coverage, store and backend checks together, and a dataset of which fixes cleared review (only with user permission).
  • False confidence: state “No guarantee of approval. App Review is Apple’s decision” on every report, and keep it in the terms. Never sell an “approved” badge. Grade findings as blockers, risks or info.
  • Marks: no Apple logos or “Apple-approved” wording. Use guideline numbers and “for App Store submissions” only descriptively. Add a non-affiliation line.
  • Security scanning ethics: scan only domains and projects the user proves they own (a DNS or file token). Never probe third-party apps.
  • Crowding: seven-plus tools priced from $2.99. Without the vibe-coder wedge this is a race to the bottom.

M.6Rough MVP plan (about 4–6 weeks for one builder; our estimate)

  • Week 1–2: repo/zip ingest; around 20 deterministic rules for Capacitor and Expo (permissions, manifest, Sign in with Apple, account deletion, WebView-only, remote code, placeholders, links). Each rule has a guideline citation and a fix-prompt template.
  • Week 3: Supabase probe behind domain verification; secrets-in-bundle check.
  • Week 4: LLM pass on screenshots and metadata with confidence labels; report page and PDF.
  • Week 5–6: Stripe paywall (free top-3, paid full report), re-scan subscription, guideline-diff watcher. Launch to r/vibecoding and builder communities with honest content (“the 2.1 checklist”).

SiblingsFive sibling concepts, ranked

2Privacy & AI-consent consistency checker ⚑

AudienceAny small app team, especially those calling OpenAI or Anthropic APIs from their app.

CoreFind the SDKs and data flows in the code. Compare them with the privacy policy text, the App Privacy “nutrition label” answers, the Play Data safety form and the privacy manifests. Check that an explicit consent screen exists before data goes to third-party AI (5.1.2(i), Nov 2025).

DemandApple cites over 443,000 privacy-violation rejections in 2025. Legal was the second-largest rejection section at 495,673.

PaymentComparable: AppClearance at $29/mo includes generated, hosted policy pages. Suggested: $15 one-time consistency report, $9/mo drift watch.

MaintenanceSDK-to-data-type mapping table needs updates monthly; it is otherwise automated. ⚑ This is preparation, not legal advice.

Risk / buildAppClearance overlaps on policy hosting. Build it from the main concept’s SDK detector plus an LLM comparison against the policy.

Apple Newsroom · 5.1.2(i) update · AppClearance

3Backend exposure scanner for vibe-coded apps

AudienceLovable, Bolt and Replit builders on Supabase or Firebase.

CoreVerified-owner scan of the deployed app: anon-key table reads, RLS gaps, service keys in the bundle, open edge functions, missing security headers.

DemandCVE-2025-48757 found 303 exposed endpoints across 170 projects.

PaymentVAS charges $19/mo and $39/mo. Lovable’s built-in scans are free. Suggested: include it in #1 instead of selling it alone.

MaintenanceLow: fixed checks, plus updates when Supabase changes its defaults.

Risk / buildThe most crowded of the six, with free incumbents. Scanning must be limited to verified owners. Build: about 2 weeks.

CVE statement · VAS · Lovable security

4Google Play pre-check + 12-tester readiness

AudienceFirst-time Android publishers on new personal accounts.

CoreAn AAB and listing scan (target SDK, permissions, Data safety vs. SDKs). It also tracks the rule that personal accounts created after Nov 13, 2023 need a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 continuous days before applying for production access.

DemandA hard, documented Google gate. Google says it blocked more than 1.75M policy-violating apps in 2025.

PaymentAppflight already runs Android checks for free. Suggested: $9 for the tester-clock tracker and production-application checklist. Don’t sell testers; trading installs risks Google’s policies.

MaintenanceLow to medium: Play policy changes several times a year.

Risk / buildThin pricing power. Best as an Android tab inside #1.

Play Console Help: testing requirements · AskSami (Google 2025 figure) · Appflight

5Payments & IAP rule checker by storefront ⚑

AudienceApps with paywalls, especially those built with Stripe-first web tools.

CoreFind Stripe or web-checkout links and digital-goods paywalls. Map them per storefront: US external links allowed since May 2025 under 3.1.1(a); elsewhere, IAP or the regional entitlement rules apply. Flag likely 3.1.1 problems.

DemandBusiness-section rejections: 283,820 in 2025. The rules changed through court orders, which causes confusion.

PaymentNo direct priced competitor found. Suggested: $19 report, $5/mo rule-change alerts.

MaintenanceThe highest of the six. Rules move with litigation and regional regulation (EU), so a human must review each change. ⚑ Not legal advice.

Risk / buildWrong advice costs a rejection or worse. Ship it narrow (US vs. everywhere else) and cite the guideline text verbatim.

Apple May 2025 update · FKKS · Transparency Report

6Accessibility pre-check for app-store labels

AudienceTeams that want to claim Apple’s Accessibility Nutrition Labels honestly, or that sell into the EU (the European Accessibility Act took effect June 28, 2025).

CoreStatic and screenshot checks: missing accessibility labels, Dynamic Type support, contrast, tap-target size. The output is a “can you honestly claim VoiceOver / Larger Text?” report.

DemandWeaker today. Apple says the labels are voluntary for now and will become required for new submissions over time, with no date.

PaymentNo cited direct comparable. Suggested: $15 per report.

MaintenanceLow. Real conformance still needs human testing; the tool must say so.

Risk / buildDemand jumps if Apple sets a date. Park it as a module and switch it on then.

Apple: Accessibility Nutrition Labels · WWDC25 session · EAA guide

Gappy’s take

The gap isn’t “a checker.” There are plenty of those. It’s a checker that speaks vibe coder: it starts from a Lovable link, finds the wrapper, the leaky table and the missing AI-consent screen at once, and hands back prompts the builder can paste. Promise preparation, never approval.
