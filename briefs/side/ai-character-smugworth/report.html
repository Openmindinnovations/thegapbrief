SummaryBottom line

1Build an uncanny "signature-look" character with one recurring bit, and label it as AI

The AI characters that took off this fall don't try to pass as human. They have one exaggerated, instantly readable silhouette and get dropped into whatever format is trending that day. Higgsfield says so itself: "A flawless face is the fastest way for a character to read as generic AI" (Semafor, Oct 7). Our proposal is Professor Smugworth: a lanky, wild-haired, insufferably confident "genius" in a lab coat over a loud suit who explains everything, is wrong half the time, and never admits it. He fits the format, he's original, and the name came up clean in a light search.

2.2Why this type wins, and what to take from it

  • Readable in one frame. Each winner has one silhouette you'd recognize as a thumbnail (mustache, bob, suit on a dog). Wild hair and a lab coat give us that.
  • The format does the work. Farquaad "just dances ... in whatever format is trending that day" (Top5Apps). The character is constant and the trend changes.
  • A personality beats a pretty face. Candy works because combat sports is "a niche full of big personalities" (Starbelo). An obnoxious know-it-all gives us a voice and a recurring joke, not just a look.
  • Transparency is safer. Openly AI accounts avoid Instagram's reach limits (see Risks).
  • Most new personas still flop. Lightreel cites one at 956 followers and a 175-view median, so plan for volume and iteration. Lightreel

  • Video generation, including Seedance 2.5 scenes, plus upscaling and scene-by-scene video analysis (YouTube link or upload).
  • TikTok: connect an account, browse TikTok's trending Commercial Music Library, and publish through a review form.

CharacterThe character: Professor Smugworth

4.1Visual references (archetypes, not people)

He draws on the long-running "absent-minded professor" and "mad scientist" archetypes: wild hair, chalk dust and a lab coat. Wikipedia notes Einstein's "expressive face and distinctive hairstyles have been widely copied" in that archetype. We use the archetype only. No Einstein face, no Doc Brown, no Rick Sanchez, no Bill Nye. Einstein's name and likeness are commercially licensed by an agent for the Hebrew University, so stay well clear.

Wikipedia · The Guardian

4.2Face, age and build

  • Age: reads as 38–45, "young enough to be annoying, old enough to be smug."
  • Build: very tall and lanky, with a slightly too-long neck and long fingers he steeples constantly. That's a deliberate Higgsfield-style exaggeration so he reads as a character, not a real person.
  • Face: long, narrow face with high cheekbones and a sharp pointed nose. Permanently raised left eyebrow, thin lips locked in a half-smirk, a small gap in the front teeth, faint chalk smudge on one cheek.
  • Eyes: unusually pale ice-grey and a little too wide, behind small perfectly round wire glasses perched low on the nose so he looks over them.
  • Skin and detail: slightly pale with ink stains on the fingertips. Clean-shaven except for long, sharply angled sideburns.

4.3Hair: wild version and tamer version

  • Wild (signature): a tall, electrified copper-orange mane that shoots upward and backward like he just touched a Van de Graaff generator, with one stubborn curl that falls over his forehead and a single white streak on the left. It should look dramatic in silhouette.
  • Tamer ("conference mode"): the same copper hair slicked back hard with visible comb lines, the white streak showing and the forehead curl still escaping. Use it for "serious" episodes and sponsor reads.
  • Running gag: the hair gets wilder as he gets more wrong or more angry, so it works as a visual meter.

4.4Expressions and mannerisms

  • Expressions: the over-the-glasses stare; the slow condescending blink; a smug closed-eye nod; a sudden manic grin when he has an "idea"; a frozen, eye-twitching smile when he's proven wrong.
  • Mannerisms: steepled fingers; pushing his glasses up with one finger before correcting someone; tapping the chalkboard twice (signature move); a little bow after saying something wrong; sniffing a beaker before drinking coffee out of it.

4.5Voice and catchphrase style

  • Voice: a crisp, nasal tenor with over-pronounced consonants and dramatic pauses. Mid-Atlantic, "lecture hall" cadence. Speeds up when excited and drops to a whisper for "the important part." Build it with a synthetic voice; never clone a real person's voice.
  • Catchphrase style: short and repeatable, starting with a correction.
    • "Ah. Ah-ah-ah. Actually." (the opener)
    • "Peer reviewed. By me." (sign-off)
    • "I'm not wrong. I'm early."
    • "Write that down. No, the other hand."

4.6Outfits and props

  • Signature outfit: a knee-length white lab coat with singed cuffs and pockets stuffed with pens, worn over a mustard-and-burgundy windowpane three-piece suit, a teal bow tie, purple socks visible above scuffed oxblood brogues, and a pocket protector that reads "PhD (pending)."
  • Alternate 1, "Gala Genius": a velvet emerald tuxedo jacket, black turtleneck and round gold glasses. For award-show parodies and press conferences.
  • Alternate 2, "Field Work": an orange hazmat suit with the hood down so the wild hair explodes out, plus aviator goggles on his forehead.
  • Alternate 3, "Off Duty": a cardigan with elbow patches over a T-shirt reading "I ♥ BEING RIGHT," plus corduroys. For casual formats and Lives.
  • Signature props: a pointer that's slightly too long; a smoking beaker he drinks coffee from; a pocket chalkboard; a tiny brass bell he rings when someone is wrong; and a lab rat sidekick named "Dr. Cheese" (non-speaking, unimpressed).

4.7Color palette

  • Copper hair #C4602D · lab-coat white #F4F1EA · mustard #D9A21B · burgundy #7A1F2B · teal accent #1F8A8A · chalkboard green #2F4F3A.
  • Use teal and copper on thumbnails and the profile picture so the account is recognizable at a glance.

4.8Personality bible

  • Core: a self-declared genius with total confidence and patchy accuracy. He's obnoxious the way a sitcom character is, smug and pompous, so the joke is always on him.
  • Backstory: claims three PhDs ("two pending, one spiritual"), was "asked to leave" every university, and runs the "Smugworth Institute," which is his garage.
  • What he says: corrections, fake equations, over-explanations of simple things, backhanded compliments ("Impressive, for a non-genius"), and grudging praise for Dr. Cheese.
  • What he never says: "I was wrong" (only "the data was rude"). Nothing punching down at real groups or real people. No medical, financial or legal advice presented as real. No politics, slurs or anything sexual. No real brand put-downs, and he never pretends to be human or a real expert.
  • Weakness that makes him likable: scared of pigeons, loses to Dr. Cheese at chess, and secretly reads every comment.

RisksRisks and rules

7.1AI labeling

  • Instagram: since Aug 31, 2026, profiles featuring an AI-generated person should switch on the "AI-generated profile" label. Unlabeled ones may lose recommendations. Instagram
  • TikTok: requires labeling realistic AI content, through the toggle or on-screen text, and may auto-label C2PA-tagged uploads permanently. TikTok says the label alone doesn't reduce distribution. TikTok · TikTok Shop rules
  • X: say "AI character" in the bio and on posts.

7.2Impersonation, likeness and copyright

  • TikTok bans using AI to imitate another person's likeness or voice, or to use someone else's name or trademarks without authorization. TikTok
  • Borrowed characters are fragile. Farquaad's account "hands the series' future to a studio" that is suing AI model companies. Top5Apps
  • Copying a format is normal; re-uploading someone's clip, cloning their face or lifting their jokes isn't. AI personas lifting comedians' sets drew public callouts this fall. Fanslist
  • For Motion Control, use Jacob's own self-filmed motion or footage we have rights to, never someone else's viral clip as the reference.
  • Use licensed music only (TikTok's Commercial Music Library) for anything branded or sponsored.

7.3Copycats

Successful characters get cloned within days. Jean Phil copycats reached 147.6K and 21K followers. Lock the handles, keep dated records of first use, and consider a trademark once the name is in use. Fully AI images may have limited copyright protection, so the name and brand carry more of the protection. UNILAD · Evans Fox LLP
