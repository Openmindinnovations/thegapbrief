3Disclosure and likeness rules

3.1Label realistic AI content on both platforms

TikTok requires creators to label AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic people or scenes, using the AI-generated label, a caption or a sticker. It may also auto-label content that carries Content Credentials. Meta applies an "AI info" label when it detects industry signals or the creator discloses. Creators must disclose photorealistic AI video and realistic-sounding AI audio, and Meta can add penalties for not doing so.

TikTok: AI-generated content · TikTok newsroom: AI labels · Meta: labeling AI content

3.2Likeness: only your own face and voice, or an original character

ElevenLabs' professional clones are limited to your own voice. HeyGen's custom avatar is built from "your own voice and likeness." TikTok's integrity rules ban impersonation. The safe pattern is an original, clearly AI character, or yourself with consent on file. Never copy a real person's face or voice. Compliance

4Instagram and TikTok: the official APIs and their limits

Plain statement: this report does not include, and Scout will not provide, methods to evade or bypass Instagram's or TikTok's bot detection: no fingerprint spoofing, proxy rotation, account farms, or "safe" action rates for unofficial bots. These methods break the platforms' terms, and enforcement can take down every linked account. The legitimate ceiling is the official API limits below.

4.1Instagram Graph API: 50 posts per 24 hours

Publishing works only for Instagram professional (Business/Creator) accounts, and media must be on a public URL. The content_publishing_limit endpoint reports quota_total = 50 containers per quota_duration of 86,400 seconds (24 hours). One older line in Meta's publishing guide says 100, so check the endpoint before you schedule. Meta recommends that apps enforce the limit themselves.

IG User Content Publishing Limit · Content Publishing guide

4.2TikTok Content Posting API: about 15 posts a day, private until audited

Unaudited apps can post for up to 5 users per 24 hours, only to private accounts, and only as SELF_ONLY. Even audited apps have a per-creator cap, "typically around 15 posts per day," shared across every app that uses Direct Post. Rate limits: 6 requests a minute per token on /post/publish/video/init/ and 20 a minute on creator_info. The error codes spam_risk_too_many_posts and spam_risk_user_banned_from_posting show where the spam lines are. Apps must show the creator's own privacy options and honor their choice.

TikTok Content Sharing Guidelines · Direct Post reference · Query Creator Info

4.3What gets accounts flagged

TikTok bans fake engagement, which covers buying or trading views, likes, follows or comments and services that artificially boost metrics. It also bans spam, impersonation, and accounts made to evade enforcement, and it removes inflated metrics. Meta's Inauthentic Behavior policy covers fake accounts, fake likes, follows and comments, and automated accounts used to deceive or evade enforcement. Instagram's developer policy has long banned "like," "share," "comment" or "follower" exchange programs. Posting AI content isn't the problem; faking engagement is.

4.4Zernio: compliant scheduling you already use

Zernio is a REST scheduling API for Instagram, TikTok and other networks, built on official connections. Pricing is per connected account: the first 2 are free, accounts 3–10 cost $6 each, 11–100 cost $3 each, and 101+ cost $1 each. Every account gets unlimited posts, analytics, comments and DMs. The platform caps in 4.1 and 4.2 still apply underneath.

5Sites like Instagress: what happened to auto-like/follow services

5.1Instagress (shut down April 20, 2017)

Instagress was a paid web bot that automatically liked and commented on posts to gain followers. It closed "by request of Instagram" and offered refunds. Instagram didn't comment on the app but pointed to its developer policy, which bans like/comment/follower exchange programs and spam.
