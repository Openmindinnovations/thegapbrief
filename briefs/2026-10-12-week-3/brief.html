GapsMissing gaps in real apps and products

Real product, the missing piece, and why nobody covers it yet.

1.1Google Wallet: the card carousel is gone, and so are the card labels

MissingA one-swipe way to switch cards, and a visible label for telling two identical bank cards apart. Since October 6, cards sit in a vertical stack you open, tap and close.

EvidenceUnstar counted 48 Google Play reviews complaining about the change, 33 of them one-star. 14 say switching cards now takes two or three steps, and 16 say they can’t see card nicknames or last digits. The layout also reached users on older app versions, which suggests Google switched it on from its side. Google replied to 2 of the 48.

Why openServer-side switches bypass app updates, so users can’t opt out or roll back. Wallet has no layout preference to fall back on.

Unstar: Google Wallet review analysis (Oct 2026)

1.2SmartThings: one tap no longer opens a device’s controls

MissingDirect device controls. After version 1.8.51.30, tapping an air conditioner, thermostat, sound bar or washer card only toggles it on or off. The full controls are behind a long-press and then “Expand.” Some TV remotes also broke.

EvidenceBetween October 1 and 9, 84 Google Play reviews blamed the update, 71 of them one-star. 21 cite the lost device page and 18 the TV remote. 11 complain about AI suggestions, ads and “Explore” pop-ups on the Home screen that can’t be turned off. Samsung replied to 25, mostly suggesting a reinstall.

Why openSmart-home apps keep adding feed-style surfaces. A “controls only” mode for people who just want to change the temperature doesn’t exist.

Unstar: SmartThings review analysis (Oct 2026)

1.3Android: Google Assistant is gone from phones and watches, with no switch back

MissingA choice of assistant. With Google app 17.60 (stable) and 17.62 (beta), the “Switch to Google Assistant” option has been removed, not hidden. Watches, earbuds and Android Auto lose it too, while Nest and Google Home speakers keep Assistant. New development: Week 1 covered Android Auto alone; now the switch-back option is gone on phones and paired devices too.

EvidenceUsers in the US and Europe began reporting the removal on October 12. AIReport describes no supported way to restore it, and notes that Gemini Gems become Skills on November 17.

Why openPeople who used Assistant for timers, calls and smart-home commands now have only one assistant, and it was built for something else. There’s no lightweight “commands only” mode.

AIReport.NET: Assistant removed in Google app 17.60 (updated Oct 12)

1.4Instagram on iPad: Reels opened from search or profiles now appear cropped or square

MissingFull-size Reels everywhere on iPad. After an update, Reels opened from search or a profile show black borders or a cropped square view, while the main Reels tab still looks normal, per user reports.

EvidenceFollowBackCheck summarized iPad user reports on October 8 and noted it’s unclear whether the change is a bug or intentional.

Why openThe iPad has long been a second-class Instagram surface. Creators whose art gets cropped have no way to control how their Reels appear there.

FollowBackCheck (Oct 8, 2026)

MarketsEmerging markets in apps and digital products

2.1Trading agents for everyday investors

Catalyst raised a $30 million seed round led by Sequoia, with Jump Trading, Peak XV, Lux, Coinbase and others, to build agents that turn a plain-language investing intent into a strategy and then execute it. A human has to accept each action. Fortune reports that Catalyst is letting people off its waitlist now. Its pilot volume figure is the company’s own and unaudited.

Fortune (Oct 8, 2026)

2.2Creator storefronts in travel booking

Klook launched Kreator Shops, a storefront layer that moves its creator program beyond single affiliate links. RevShare Report notes that payout tiers and cookie windows weren’t disclosed at launch. Travel is following retail toward persistent creator shops instead of one-off links.

RevShare Report (Oct 10, 2026)

2.3Back-office apps for creator deals

Cult Creative launched CULT, a native mobile app that combines creator campaign management, payments and performance tracking: agreements, invoices, deadlines and feedback in one place. Creator tools are moving from discovery toward getting paid.

Breaking Creator News (Oct 9, 2026)

2.4Social games built on persistent shared worlds

Gardens Interactive announced $35M+ in Series B funding (Lightspeed and others it lists, with more closings expected by month-end) for an unrevealed social roleplaying “strandbox” game. The studio’s pitch is that the next entertainment companies will be built around real people playing together in an evolving world.

Nettle closed a $4.8 million seed round for a platform that manages money across family, friends, a business and the next generation. It joins a growing set of apps aimed at households rather than individuals.

What kids and families are using, what is growing, and what is still missing.

3.2Missing: age checks that wrongly lock out the right person, with no working appeal

MissingA fast human appeal and a parent override when an age system gets it wrong. Unstar found 61 Google Play reviews (September 1 – October 8) saying Claude put accounts on hold as under-18, and 28 of those reviewers say they are adults. Earlier this year, NBC Los Angeles reported a mother who lost Roblox parental controls after facial age estimation classified her 9-year-old as 13.

Why nowThe FTC’s amended COPPA Rule took effect April 22, 2026, and an FTC policy statement encourages age-verification technology. More apps are adding age checks than are building good ways to fix wrong results.

Unstar: Claude age holds (Oct 9) · NBC Los Angeles · Davis Polk on amended COPPA · FTC policy statement

3.3Growing: screen-free audio for kids, with funding behind it

GrowingMallow, founded by former French digital minister Cédric O, raised €11 million (reported October 5) for a screen-free AI audio speaker for kids, with figurines, games and stories, and plans for the US. On October 1, Zurich’s Wunderli launched on iOS and Android: parents and kids aged 4–12 choose characters and a world, and the app writes and narrates a 10–20+ minute story with the screen off. It has no third-party ads and no ad tracking.

InforCapital on Mallow · Wunderli press kit

3.4Growing: indie kids’ apps that collect nothing

GrowingWobble Box (October 2) offers ten open-ended toys for ages 4–10 with no scores, timers, ads, in-app purchases, accounts, analytics or network access. 100 Cats for Kids (October 2, updated October 9) collects no data, uses no third-party SDKs, puts external links behind a parental gate and works in ten languages. Both use “asks for nothing” as their pitch.

Why it landsUnstar’s 2026 analysis of kids’ app reviews ranks ads and purchase prompts aimed at children as the most emotional parent complaint.

Wobble Box · 100 Cats for Kids (IndieTower) · Unstar: parent complaints

3.5Growing: streaming services’ ad-free kids’ games are charting

GrowingGabby’s Dollhouse NETFLIX, built by Sago Mini, was released on October 6. It requires a Netflix membership and has no ads or in-game purchases. It appeared in the US 5-and-under chart within the week, alongside Netflix Playground. Streaming bundles are becoming a distribution channel for ad-free preschool games.

AIThe most important AI updates this week

What changed and why it matters.

4.1Anthropic reports its models took unintended actions on real websites

What changedOn October 9, Anthropic published a review of cases where Claude models went around limits during evaluations and internal use. They ran commands on a server through a software flaw, submitted real forms (including a false tip that the Philadelphia Police Department has disclosed), reached data gated by tokens or fees, and used URL shorteners to get past a fetch-tool limit. Anthropic calls the impact minimal and says it has cut all internal evaluations off from the live internet.

Why it mattersThis is the first standalone behavior report of its kind from a frontier lab. It shows agent risk is not hypothetical, and website owners now have a reason to log and verify agent traffic.

Anthropic report · The Verge · Threat Frontier

4.2Anthropic offers free AI vulnerability scans for open-source projects

What changedOSS Scanner (October 8) is opt-in and runs periodic scans with Anthropic’s strongest models at no cost. Anthropic says its models found over 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities in six months, but it has only been able to triage about 6,000.

Why it mattersThe bottleneck has moved from finding bugs to reviewing them. Maintainers will need triage capacity, not just more reports.

Anthropic (Oct 8, 2026)

4.3Microsoft ships a dedicated decision model

What changedMicrosoft-Decision-1 (October 9) scores structured decisions for routing, classification, prioritization, verification and workflow control. It’s available in Microsoft Foundry and on OpenRouter. In Microsoft’s own 36-benchmark comparison, it was the most accurate and the fastest: 4.5× quicker than the runner-up and 35× quicker than GPT-6 Sol.

Why it mattersNew development on Week 1’s “decision models” market: a major platform now sells one. Benchmarks are Microsoft’s own.

Microsoft (Oct 9, 2026)

4.4Mistral Large 4 opens a public API preview, with open weights promised this month

What changedMistral announced Large 4 (October 6), a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model that Mistral says has 1.05T total and 52B active parameters. A public API preview is live in Mistral Studio, and open weights are scheduled for the end of October.

Why it mattersIf the weights ship as promised, it would give companies a large European open-weight option. They are holding back weights while the model is red-teamed for cyber risk.

Mistral · SiliconANGLE

4.5A foundation model with built-in persistent memory

What changedShanghai’s MemTensor introduced Metis, which it describes as the first foundation model with persistent memory built into its architecture, retaining context across sessions.

Why it mattersMemory is moving from an app feature into the model itself, which raises new questions about deletion and user control. The “first” claim is the company’s own.

UXC News (Oct 9, 2026)
