Should Rich Buy a Kenworth Truck and Trailer? Costs, Pay and King County Work in the Pipeline

What a Kenworth truck-and-trailer costs to buy and run in 2026, what it can earn in King County, and where the work is coming from. Written for Rich.

Assigned by Jacob Peterson for his dad, Rich · Research. Prices checked Oct 27, 2026. Every claim links to its source; numbers appear only where a cited source states them.

SummaryBottom line for Rich

1Recommendation: buy used, line up work first, start as a sub or through a broker

The upside is real. A truck-and-trailer driver on King County public works must be paid at least $86.95 an hour (L&I, effective Sept 2, 2026), and Seattle–Tacoma private hauling is quoted at $130–$250 an hour depending on truck size. Big public jobs keep coming: West Seattle Link, I-405, SR 520, I-5 paving and King County sewer projects.

The safest way in is a good used Kenworth T880 (about $42,500–$115,000 in current WA listings) with a used pup trailer ($11,000–$16,000), not a new $318,500 truck. Before buying, get a written insurance quote (new ventures run $14,000–$20,000+ a year) and get on the call list of at least one broker or prime. Don't buy a new rig on hope; buy a used one once there's a phone number that will call you Monday morning.

FinancingLoans and down payments

3.1What lenders are quoting in 2026

  • Strong credit (650–720+ FICO) and 2+ years in business: about 7%–14% APR, 0%–10% down, 24–72 month terms.
  • Fair credit: about 12%–18% APR, 15%–20% down. Subprime: 18%–28% APR, 20%–35% down.
  • A brand-new business should plan on 10%–25% down. Ask for a seasonal payment schedule (smaller winter payments).

Rich's own credit and history will set the real rate; a startup usually lands in the middle to upper part of these ranges.

Running costsFuel, insurance, upkeep, permits, taxes

4.1Fuel

The West Coast diesel average for October 2026 is about $7.30 a gallon; the Oct 26 weekly reading was $7.329. EIA doesn't publish a separate Washington diesel price. Fuel is the cost that moves the most, so ask whether a job pays a fuel surcharge.

4.4Licenses and permits (Washington)

  • CDL Class A for the driver, since truck plus trailer is a combination vehicle. WA DOL CDL
  • WA business license / UBI number through the Department of Revenue. WA DOR business license
  • USDOT number from FMCSA (Washington requires one for intrastate commercial vehicles too). MC authority only if hauling for hire across state lines. FMCSA registration
  • IRP plates and IFTA are only needed if the truck crosses state lines. For a 105,500 lb dump truck on Washington's IRP chart, the full-year WA license fee is $3,376.20 plus a 15% freight fee ($506.43) and small filing fees. A King County-only truck registers in-state by gross weight instead. WA IRP Manual · 2026 WA IRP fee chart
  • Public works: anyone working on public jobs files L&I intents and affidavits and follows the trucking rule (WAC 296-127-018). WAC 296-127-018

4.5Taxes

A new truck pays federal excise tax on top of the sticker price (Papé's price excludes it), plus Washington sales or use tax at the local rate. Hauling for hire in Washington is generally taxed under the public utility tax rather than regular B&O. Ask a WA tax pro to confirm the rate for Rich's setup. There's also federal Heavy Vehicle Use Tax (Form 2290) for trucks 55,000 lb and up.

EarningsWhat the truck can earn

5.1Public works floor: $86.95/hr

L&I's King/Pierce/Snohomish rate for Truck Driver, Dump Truck & Trailer is $58.43 base + $28.52 fringe = $86.95/hr, effective Sept 2, 2026. That is the driver's minimum wage on public jobs, not what the truck rents for. Equipment is paid on top in the hourly rate a prime pays a hauler. Rates lock at the bid date, and L&I's next update is due Feb 1, 2027.

5.2Private market hourly rates

Seattle–Tacoma planning ranges for 2026: tandem $130–$180/hr, tri-axle $160–$220/hr, quad/super dump $180–$250/hr, usually with a two-hour minimum. Night export-soil work downtown reportedly pays 10–15% more. We found no published Seattle rate specifically for truck-and-pup or truck-and-transfer, so get a quote sheet from a local broker.

5.3Own authority vs. working through a broker

  • Own authority: keeps the full rate but pays top-tier insurance and has to find work and collect payment.
  • Broker or hauling company: companies like Stone Resources dispatch dump trucks for import/export across King, Pierce and Snohomish. Dispatch services in the area advertise 5–8% of gross on your own authority, or 8–12% leased under theirs (Cargo Voyager). Leased-on operators typically pay much less insurance: $2,000–$5,000/yr (BulkLoads).
  • Also call the aggregate yards (Miles Sand & Gravel, Cadman) to get on their hauler lists.

At 1,500 hours the truck would gross $225,000 and clear roughly $102,000 before Rich's own pay, income taxes and any broker cut. At $130/hr, break-even rises to about 725 hours. If he hires a driver instead of driving himself, public-works pay of $86.95/hr would eat most of the margin on those jobs.

6.4How a one-truck hauler gets on these jobs

  • Sub to the primes: haulers work for the excavation and paving primes, not the agency directly. Find winning primes on bid tabs, then drop off a COI, W-9 and rate sheet at their job trailers.
  • Get certified: Washington OMWBE certifies DBE/MBE/WBE firms (DBE requires 51% ownership by a socially and economically disadvantaged owner). King County's Small Contractors & Suppliers (SCS) program is size-based, and OMWBE-certified firms get it through Fast Track. Primes need certified subs to meet project goals, so this is a real door-opener.
  • Brokers and yards: Stone Resources, area dispatch services, and gravel and asphalt suppliers keep hauler call lists.
  • Watch the portals: Sound Transit, WSDOT contracting, King County, and the Regional Contracting Forum, where primes and small subs meet.

RisksWhat can go wrong

7.1The big risks

  • Rain and seasonality: Western WA runs year-round but with rain delays, and earthwork slows in the wet months. Ask lenders for seasonal payments. source
  • Fuel swings: diesel is already over $7 on the West Coast. Push for fuel surcharges or hourly rates.
  • Slow-paying primes: subs often wait 30–60+ days or longer for payment. Keep 2–3 months of costs in cash, or use a broker that pays faster.
  • Project delays: I-405 has already slipped to 2027, and transit starts depend on procurement schedules.
  • Insurance shock: new ventures pay the most, and one claim can raise the renewal.
  • Used truck surprises: high-mile emissions systems and the engine are where the big repair bills hide.
