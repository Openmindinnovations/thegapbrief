15 Low-Barrier Creative Aftermarket Opportunities for Optimus

Costumes, shells, stands, wranglers, and photo booths for Tesla’s humanoid robot, plus an honest look at the fact that you can’t buy one yet.

Assigned by Jacob Peterson. Every factual claim links to its source. Prices are as listed by the linked sellers when checked on October 10, 2026; “suggested” prices are our proposals. No sales numbers, owner counts, or market sizes are estimated. Not affiliated with Tesla. “Tesla” and “Optimus” are Tesla’s marks and are used here only to describe the robot.

StatusWhere Optimus actually stands (October 2026)

Short version: announced, being built, not sold. Optimus robots are being made, but Tesla is keeping them. There is no consumer channel, no price, and no developer program, so most of the fun ideas below either start on robots you can buy or rent today, or wait for robots to reach homes.

S.1The comparable aftermarket that already exists

  • Unitree sells the G1 humanoid at $13,500 and the Go2 robot dog from $1,600 on its store. Its Dex3-1 hand is an optional add-on for the EDU edition, with no public price.
  • Boston Dynamics Spot has an official accessory line (Arm, Dock, Cam 2, batteries) sold by quote. The Arm lifts 11 kg, and the Dock requires a Spot Enterprise license. Third-party payloads go through a partner program.
  • Robot costumes are already a product: a Go2 “Panda Skin” lists at $1,000 (K-Robotics), and MCSDINO sells an Ankylosaurus costume shell for Go2 at $1,500, robot not included.
  • Humanoid rental is a business: ZMProbots rents a G1 from $299/day self-service, or $1,875/day with an operator. Tempo lists $2,999/day operated in the US.
  • A home humanoid you can order: 1X NEO costs $20,000 or $499/month. HumanoidStore reports that the box includes a charger and a lint roller.
  • The Tesla car aftermarket as an analogy: Tesla’s own extended service plans run $50–$150/month. Third-party chrome-delete kits cost $139–$189 DIY. Owners buy cosmetics and care plans.

How the 15 are ranked

Ranked by ease of starting now: how little capital and how few external dependencies it needs today. Start now ideas (1–9) work on robots you can already rent or buy, or need no robot at all, and can switch to Optimus later. Needs robots in homes ideas (10–15) wait for consumer units. Speculative marks ideas that depend on things Tesla hasn’t announced.

Every idea shares one rule: brand it as your own, describe compatibility honestly, and never imply Tesla’s endorsement.

MoneySponsorships and paid tiers. Suggested: $5/month supporter tier. There is no cited price comparable for this kind of content, so treat pricing as a test.

RulesGround rules for every idea

G.1Brand, safety, and timing

  • Marks: use your own name. Say “fits humanoid robots, including [model]” only where it’s true, with a non-affiliation line. No Tesla logos or lookalike styling.
  • Safety: nothing that loads joints, covers sensors, or touches batteries and electronics. Cosmetic and removable only.
  • Timing: build on Unitree, 1X, and rental robots now. Treat Optimus as an upgrade path, not a launch date, because Tesla’s consumer timeline is a conditional target.
  • Numbers: no owner counts or market sizes exist to plan against. Validate each idea with a landing page or a rental-event pilot first.
