A Coin Run by a Bot Team: Tokenomics, Holder Voting, the Cheapest Launch Path and Hot Crypto Markets

How a token built and operated by AI agents could fund its own compute, let holders vote on what the bots build next, launch for close to nothing, and earn its way onto exchanges. Plus original ideas and where crypto attention is right now.

Assigned by Jacob Peterson · Research. Prices and data checked Oct 27, 2026. Every figure links to its source. Allocation percentages and feature ideas are our own design proposals and are labeled that way. This is not legal, tax or investment advice.

SummaryBottom line

1Recommendation: a Base launch with a utility-first token, a compute treasury and Snapshot voting

Launch on Base through Clanker. You pay only gas, the liquidity is locked from day one, and Clanker's new "Droid" option pays an agent's running costs from LP fees, which matches "keep the bots working." Start governance on off-chain Snapshot (no gas for voters) and move binding treasury votes on-chain later. Skip paid CEX listings at first: build DEX volume and holders, then apply directly to MEXC, which has the lowest reported entry bar.

2Cheapest real cost to go live: gas only. A tier-2 CEX costs tens of thousands

Creating a coin on pump.fun (Solana) or Clanker/Zora (Base) has no platform fee. Reported CEX entry costs run from about $20K–$75K all-in at MEXC to $500K–$2M+ at Binance (OpenLiquid). Binance itself says it charges no listing fee and that anyone promising a listing is a fraud.

pump.fun fees · OpenLiquid MEXC guide · Binance listing warning (via MEXC News)

3Be careful with timing: AI-agent tokens are down hard and prediction markets are at records

AI-agent tokens are about $3.4–3.5B in total market cap (CoinGecko, Sharpe), down about 80% from a ~$15.7B peak in January 2025 (WooFun). The story has to be real work done on-chain, not a chatbot with a ticker.

2.1Fund compute from fees, not token sales

Treasury tokens dumped on the market to pay bills will sink the price. Pay the bots from trading-fee revenue in SOL/USDC first. On pump.fun the creator earns 0.30% of bonding-curve volume, and after graduation that share rises to as much as 0.95% at some market-cap tiers. On Base, Clanker routes LP rewards (it keeps 20% of LP fees). Its Droid feature funds an agent from LP rewards and lets anyone top up the Droid's wallet in USDC.

pump.fun fee table · Clanker fees (PoolFans docs) · Clanker Droid

2.2A public "burn meter"

Idea: post a live dashboard of the bots' monthly compute spend next to fee income, and show how many months of runway are left. If income exceeds spend, holders vote on whether the surplus goes to buyback, bounties or more bots.

3.1Suggested setup

Use quadratic voting for feature choices, so a single whale can't decide the roadmap. Use conviction voting for the monthly compute budget. Require a timelock plus a human veto on any vote that moves treasury funds, so a governance attack can't drain it.

4.2Lean sequence

1) Launch on a gas-only launchpad. 2) Get an audit if you add any custom contracts (treasury, vesting). 3) Get listed on CoinGecko and DEX screeners. 4) Grow organic holders and volume. 5) Apply directly to MEXC and Bitget, and never through a "listing agent."

IdeasWays to stand out (ideas, unverified as "first")

Everything below is our own idea. We did not find these exact combinations in our searches, but that doesn't prove nobody has built them.

5.1"Hire the next bot" votes

Holders vote to add a new agent role, such as auditor, meme artist or market-data analyst. The treasury funds its compute only while its public KPIs stay above a voted threshold, and it is "fired" automatically when they fall.

5.2Proof-of-work logs

Each bot posts a signed, hashed log of every task, commit and API bill on-chain. Holders can audit exactly what the treasury bought.

5.3Bot-vs-bot roadmap debates

Before each vote, two agents argue for and against a proposal in a public livestream, with an on-chain summary attached to the ballot.

5.4Feature futures

A small prediction market on "will feature X ship by date Y," with resolution posted by the bots' proof logs. This connects to the hottest sector right now. It is compliance-heavy (see Legal).

5.5Compute-backed floor

Surplus fees buy prepaid inference credits that holders can redeem by voting for public-good jobs, so the token stands for a claim on bot labor directed by votes.

5.6Agent-paid micro-services

The bots sell small services to other agents (data pulls, summaries) over x402-style pay-per-query rails, and the income flows to the compute treasury.

Virtuals update (x402 use)

5.7Kill switch vote

Holders can vote to pause the bots and send the remaining treasury back pro rata. This is a credible answer to "is this a rug?"

LegalLegal and risk, plainly (not legal advice)

7.1SEC: the line is "managerial efforts of others"

The SEC's March 2026 interpretation (effective Mar 23) treats meme coins as digital collectibles that are not securities, because their value comes from supply and demand. A coin that promises a team of bots will build features to raise its value looks more like an investment contract. Market it as a utility and voting token, and never promise returns. The proposed "Regulation Crypto Assets" is not final; White & Case doesn't expect a final rule before Q1 2027. The CLARITY Act is not law: Senate cloture failed 49–50 on Sep 15, 2026.

SEC release · White & Case · CryptoTicker

7.2Washington State

Washington DFI generally requires a money-transmitter license for exchanges, custodial wallets that can move funds, and virtual-currency payment processors serving residents. Keep the treasury in a non-custodial multisig and don't hold or move users' funds.

WA DFI primer

7.3Rug-pull perception and reputation

Most of the previous cycle's top AI-agent tokens are down 90–99% (WooFun), and the public assumes the worst. Mitigations: locked LP, a vested builder share, a public treasury, the kill-switch vote, a named human accountable for the project, and no paid influencers without disclosure. Run it through a separate LLC, and get a crypto lawyer to review it before launch.
