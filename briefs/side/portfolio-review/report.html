OMI Portfolio Review: Wash Wizardz, Get UI Now, DeepReach, FlirtFee and Fetch

A site-by-site review of the Open Mind Innovations companies (Kapsoul, Hivvly, iMusement, CharacterGuard and Anchor AR excluded at Jacob’s request), with prioritized fixes and proposed bot teams for FlirtFee, DeepReach and Get UI Now.

Prepared for Jacob Peterson. Observations come from fetching each public homepage on October 12, 2026 with curl (HTML, headers, link checks) and headless Chrome screenshots at 1366×900 and 390×844. No traffic, revenue or user figures were available to us; none are estimated. Where a site makes a claim we couldn’t check (reviews, client counts), we say “unverified.” That doesn’t mean it’s false; it means the page doesn’t prove it.

Common to every live site

None of the four has a privacy policy or terms page: /privacy and /terms return 404, and no footer links to one. None has robots.txt or sitemap.xml (both 404), and none sets a canonical tag or uses structured data (JSON-LD). Three of the four post their forms to Formspree, so form data goes to a third party; that alone needs a privacy policy. One shared fix, a legal-pages-and-SEO-basics pass, could be run across all four by one bot.

On the OMI portal itself: it lists FlirtFee as “Link coming,” but flirtfee.com is live as an investor pitch. Decide on purpose whether the portal should link to it (see the FlirtFee section first).

ReviewsCompany by company

1Wash Wizardz: pressure washing

What worksThe clearest offer in the portfolio. The headline “We Make Dirt Disappear Like Magic” sits next to a phone number, a 30-second quote, a $199 driveway special, “from” prices for each service, annual plans ($499 / $899 / $1,499) and an instant-estimate tool. Title and meta description are good. GA4 (gtag) and the Meta pixel are installed. The mobile layout is clean and has a sticky “Get Instant Estimate” button.

Fix first

  1. Page weight: 11.0 MB of HTML, because 17 images are embedded inline as base64. Every visitor downloads all of them before the page can render, which is slow on mobile data. Move images to files, serve WebP/AVIF and lazy-load below the fold.
  2. Back up the trust claims. The page says “4.98★ Google,” “500+ Google Reviews,” “BBB Accredited,” “Licensed • Insured” and “500+ Happy Properties.” None of these links anywhere, and no license number is shown. Link the Google profile and BBB page, show the license and insurance details, or remove any claim that isn’t true. Unbacked review claims are a regulatory risk (the FTC’s 2024 rule bans fake or misrepresented reviews) and they lower trust.
  3. Local SEO: the page text names no city or service area, apart from an 818 phone number. Add the service area, a LocalBusiness JSON-LD block, a Google Business Profile link and a page per city or service.
  4. Add privacy and terms pages (the site has a quote form and tracking pixels), an og:image, robots.txt and a sitemap.

2Get UI Now: fast app and web design

What worksFast and light (99.5 KB, about 0.37 s to fetch). The pain is stated clearly (“agencies charge $5k–$15k”), the tiers are simple ($99 / $399 / $799+), and the 3-step process and portfolio are good. Most Open Graph tags are present.

Fix first

  1. There’s no way to pay. Every “Get Started” button leads to a quote form, and the pricing note says packages are “starting points.” At least the $99 single-screen tier should go straight to checkout (a Stripe link plus a brief form). That’s the cheapest way to learn whether people will buy.
  2. The quote form asks too much. Phone is required (“for our follow-up call”), plus screen count and project type. Make phone optional and state the turnaround (we found a 24-hour estimate promise, but no delivery time).
  3. Broken and unclear links. The “Built with v0” badge returns 404 and floats over the mobile layout. The footer says “Powered by Lumina Studio” with no explanation. Remove the badge and either explain the studio or drop the line.
  4. No analytics found (no GA, Plausible, PostHog or similar in the HTML), so the funnel can’t be measured. Add privacy and terms pages, an og:image, a sitemap and robots.txt. “Real Designs That Launched Real Products” and “Trusted by indie founders” need links or names, or softer wording.

3DeepReach: AI-assisted marketing packages

What worksSix clear monthly packages ($399 / $995 / $1,195 / $1,995) with deliverables, each linking straight to a Stripe Payment Link. That’s a real payment path. There’s a strategy-call CTA, GA4, Segment and the Meta pixel, and a solid title and meta description.

Fix first

  1. Verify or remove the proof. The page claims “Trusted by 150+ growing businesses,” “Helping brands grow since 2020,” “94% client retention,” “3.2x avg. engagement,” “47% avg. open-rate lift,” a logo row (Bloom Wellness, TechFix Solutions, Coastal Realty, Northline Co., Peak Fitness) and three named testimonials. We can’t verify any of them. If they’re placeholders, remove them now: the FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials (effective October 2024) allows civil penalties. Replace them with real, permissioned results, even one.
  2. Remove “Email scraping and lead list building” from the $1,995 package. Scraped lists and cold email collide with CAN-SPAM and, for EU/UK contacts, GDPR. Offer “compliant lead research” instead.
  3. Mobile layout. In our 390-px render the hero text is clipped at the left edge, the menu icon draws as a single line, and a sticky “Book a Free Strategy Call” button overlaps “View Packages.” Check this on a real phone.
  4. Page weight: 8.1 MB with 35 inline base64 images. Same fix as Wash Wizardz.
  5. The “Growth Projector” shows +85% revenue and +113% ROI. Even labeled “illustrative,” that reads as a promise. Remove it or base it on real client data. Add terms covering the 3-month minimum and cancellation next to the Stripe buttons, plus a privacy page, og tags, a sitemap and robots.txt.

4FlirtFee: proximity “pay to connect” marketplace (investor page)

What it isflirtfee.com is a single “Investor Pitch” page, not a product. The concept: a live map of people within about 5,000 ft. “Hosts” set prices to unlock their Instagram ($7), TikTok ($9), phone number ($22) or a 30-minute video call ($38, host must approve). The platform takes about 28–30%, and message packs cost $6. The page asks for $20,000–$30,000 on a SAFE through an “Invest Now / Reserve Your Investment” form (Formspree) that collects name, email, phone and amount.

Fix first

  1. Securities: talk to counsel before leaving this public. A public page soliciting investment amounts is a general solicitation. The footer says “For accredited investors only” and “Not a solicitation,” which contradicts the “Invest Now” buttons. If the raise relies on Rule 506(c), investors’ accredited status must be verified; 506(b) generally doesn’t allow public advertising. Until counsel weighs in, put the page behind a password or replace it with a waitlist.
  2. Remove uncited or made-up figures: “73% of young adults…,” “4 in 5 say…,” “Projected 1.4M monthly active users,” “40+ cities by 2028,” and the host example “Ava Chen… $2,840 earned.” Only the map is labeled “stylized.” In an investor context, unsupported numbers are a liability.
  3. Trust and safety is the product. Selling a nearby stranger’s phone number on a live map raises stalking, harassment, minors and solicitation risks that app stores and payment processors screen for. Unlocked contact info is also likely a digital good under Apple’s in-app purchase rule (3.1.1), which affects the fee math. This has to be designed before any build (see the bot plan).
  4. Fix the character-encoding bugs (“Â© 2026,” “Ã—” on the close button). “Privacy · Terms · Legal” is plain text, not links; add the pages. No analytics and no robots.txt or sitemap.

DeepReachEdits, a bot-run marketing plan and a team

D.1Specific edits (site and positioning)

  • Pick a niche. “Small and growing businesses” is everyone. Lead with one vertical where proof can be built fast, such as local home-service businesses. Wash Wizardz is in-house and could become the first real, permissioned case study.
  • Lead with the honest AI angle: “Marketing run by an AI team with a human strategist on every account.” That’s distinctive and true to how DeepReach would actually operate.
  • Replace the proof block with only verified items (see fix #1 above). Until then, sell a free 10-minute marketing audit (a sample report) instead of “Trusted by 150+.”
  • Add a cheaper first purchase before the 3-month minimum, such as a one-time setup or audit sprint (suggested price; untested), so buyers can try the service with less risk.
  • Remove email scraping and the Growth Projector. Add terms, privacy, og:image, a sitemap and robots.txt. Compress images. Fix the mobile hero and the sticky-button overlap.

Get UI NowEdits, a bot-run marketing plan and a team

G.1Specific edits (site and positioning)

  • Sharpen the buyer. The highest-intent buyer is the founder about to build with Lovable, Bolt, Cursor or Rork, who wants real designed screens before prompting. Headline idea: “Pro app screens in 48 hours, ready to hand to your AI builder.” Only promise 48 hours if you can deliver it.
  • Sell the $99 screen directly: checkout first, a brief form after. Keep quotes for larger tiers. State the delivery time per tier.
  • Proof: turn the six portfolio tiles into short case cards (brief → screens → what launched, with a link), or relabel them as “sample work.”
  • Remove the v0 badge and the unexplained “Lumina Studio” line. Make phone optional. Add analytics, privacy and terms (the form collects phone numbers), og:image, a sitemap and robots.txt.

Gappy’s take

All four sites share a pattern: a strong offer up top, but thin proof and missing basics underneath. One “basics bot” pass (legal pages, sitemap, og image, image compression, analytics) would lift every site at once. After that, the order is FlirtFee’s legal check, DeepReach’s claims cleanup, Get UI Now’s checkout, and Wash Wizardz’s speed.
